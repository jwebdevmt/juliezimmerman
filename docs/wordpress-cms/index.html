Philosophy

Engineering Philosophy

A CMS succeeds when editors can use it confidently and engineers can still understand it years later.

A reliable CMS starts with clear content models and predictable behavior.

  • Customization should solve a real editorial or operational problem.
  • Accessibility, security, and maintainability belong in the architecture.
  • Fleet-scale work requires standards, repeatable checks, and planned retirement paths.

Approach

How I Work

I begin with the publishing workflow and the system's actual constraints. I map the content model, dependencies, integrations, and failure points before choosing implementation details.

At fleet scale, one-off fixes become liabilities. I favor reusable patterns, clear ownership, conservative dependencies, and tooling that makes drift visible.

Business Value

Why durable CMS architecture matters

Most CMS costs arrive after launch: fragile updates, unclear ownership, inaccessible interfaces, and custom behavior no one can safely change.

A well-structured system lowers those costs by making routine work predictable and unusual failures diagnosable.
