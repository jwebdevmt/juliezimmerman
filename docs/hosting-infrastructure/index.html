Hosting & Infrastructure

Operating web infrastructure with standards, visibility, and an exit plan.

I manage hosting as an operational system: environments, DNS, SSL, backups, deployment, updates, capacity, lifecycle, and incident response across more than 250 active web properties.

That experience led me to formalize managed-hosting standards around supportability and planned obsolescence rather than allowing every environment to become a permanent exception.

Philosophy

Engineering Philosophy

Infrastructure is reliable when routine work is predictable and exceptional work is recoverable.

Every environment needs a known owner, purpose, and lifecycle.

  • Backups matter only when restoration is understood and tested.
  • Deployment and update practices should reduce drift.
  • Unsupported infrastructure is a business risk, not merely a technical inconvenience.

Unsupported infrastructure is a business risk, not merely a technical inconvenience.

Problems

Problems I Solve

The most valuable work usually begins where a system has accumulated complexity, operational risk, or assumptions no one has revisited.

  • Fleet-wide hosting consistency
  • DNS and SSL transitions
  • Backups, restoration, and migration
  • Unsupported runtime and plugin retirement
  • Performance and capacity issues
  • Launch and post-launch operational QA

Approach

How I Work

I standardize the repeatable parts while documenting legitimate exceptions. That creates enough consistency to detect drift without pretending every workload is identical.

Infrastructure decisions include the eventual migration or retirement path, because systems that cannot change safely become liabilities.
